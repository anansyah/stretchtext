StretchText

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Stretchtext is a hypertext feature that lets a reader expand a passage where they want more detail, without leaving the page or following a link. Ted Nelson set out the idea as early as 1967 and named it in 1970, describing two throttles: one for navigation, one for expansion. The reader's click is the only gesture needed, and the text keeps its place while it grows. and shrink it back where they don't.

Writing every level by hand was too much work, so it never caught on outside hypertext research and electronic literature. Works such as Pry, Victory Garden and The Jew's Daughter used it as a narrative device, letting a reader open a memory or a medical chart in place. Each of those levels had to be authored separately, which is why the technique stayed a curiosity for decades. Generated text makes the extra levels cheap enough to be worth writing.
